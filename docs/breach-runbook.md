# Personal data incident runbook — draft for counsel

19 November 2026. Private prototype, not an approved incident policy. PDPA section
23 delegates notification details/time to rules; the inspected publisher listing
includes a consultation draft. **No statutory notification period is asserted.**
Have counsel verify applicable current rules, duties and commencement. A generic
foreign-law 72-hour period must not be silently substituted.

1. **Contain promptly.** Stop the affected local app/gateway, preserve the
   running configuration identity and prevent further processing. Do not connect
   to the home-lab/production or add network access to diagnose an incident. Stop
   only owned services; do not indiscriminately delete evidence or other stacks.
2. **Escalate internally.** Notify the actual accountable controller/privacy lead
   and qualified counsel through their approved channel. Roles/contacts remain
   UNASSIGNED in this draft. Do not paste query content, identities, credentials,
   screenshots or raw logs into Git, chat, monitoring labels or a public ticket.
3. **Establish minimum facts.** Record detection time, affected component/version,
   data categories, approximate subjects/records, recipients/locality, exposure
   window, protections and containment. Distinguish known facts, estimates and
   unknowns. Avoid collecting raw scenarios to prove what was intentionally not
   retained. Restrict/encrypt any necessary incident record under counsel's
   evidence-preservation and retention decision.
4. **Assess harm and obligations immediately with counsel.** Consider children,
   distress/violence allegations, professional privilege, onward disclosure,
   encryption/key compromise and whether the issue affects source publications
   or runtime facts. Verify section 23 rules and any other applicable duties;
   determine who must notify whom, when, how and with what content. The controller
   owns the decision. Never wait for a made-up timer before seeking counsel.
5. **Prepare a reviewable notification if required.** Include responsible contact,
   incident nature, categories/approximate numbers, likely effects, measures and
   practical protective guidance approved by safeguarding/legal reviewers. Use
   a minimal, verified recipient/channel. This repository sends no notification
   automatically; actual outreach requires the operator's explicit direction.
6. **Recover safely.** Rotate only affected dedicated development credentials;
   preserve their values outside logs/chat. Rebuild from pinned artefacts, test
   no-egress/logging again, restore an authenticated encrypted source backup into
   a fresh isolated database, compare logical checksums, and confirm no query
   history has appeared. Do not restore compromised keys into the same backup
   set or overwrite unrelated volumes.
7. **Review and retain proportionately.** Record decisions/notification evidence,
   corrective actions, responsible roles and follow-up dates. Approve a specific
   restricted retention period, including legal holds if justified. Reassess
   DPIA/DPMP/notice and launch gates before resuming. Delete excess incident data
   only under the approved evidence policy; normal seven-day operations cleanup
   must not be confused with an incident legal-hold decision.

Run tabletop exercises with invented facts only: accidental query-bearing debug
log, outbound model call, lost encrypted backup plus key, malicious browser
extension and misrouted institutional intake. Record pass/fail/actions by role,
not personal details. None of these hypothetical events is reported as an actual
incident in T7.

Counsel questions: current final breach instruments/time/thresholds; effective
date and reach; controller/processor/public-authority roles; contact channels;
special-category/minor/privilege implications; preserving evidence without new
unnecessary disclosure; notifications to subjects and Authority; contractual
institution duties and proportionate deletion/hold schedules.
